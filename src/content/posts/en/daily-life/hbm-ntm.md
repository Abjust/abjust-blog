## Table of contents

## Where the Dream Began
In early September 2026, with nothing better to do, I started yet another survival world to play on my own.

I went in intending to beat it, but I had no idea whether I'd give up halfway like last time.

## Taking Shape
At first there weren't many machines at home, so a simple combustion generator was enough to keep the power on. It only ran at 50% efficiency, but it held up.

Back then my main machines were the assembly machine, the chemical plant and the shredder. The shredder breaks ores into smaller pieces, which increases yield.

Later, as I progressed, power usage kept growing, until I had no choice but to bring in a solar tower boiler.
After that I combined a thermal power station with the solar tower boiler: solar power during the day, burning fuel to get through the night.

The thermal power station uses a boiler to put the heat from burning fuel to work, boiling water into steam. A steam turbine then turns the steam into electricity at 100% efficiency.

![The solar tower boiler and the new site of the thermal power station, later switched to industrial steam turbines and hooked up to new power lines](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/solar_tower_boiler.png)

## The Oil Industry Sets Off
In real life, oil is called "the lifeblood of the economy". In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, oil is just as important a resource.

To progress in the game, there's no getting around polymer bars. In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, 2 coal powder, 1 fluorite and 1000 mB of petroleum gas (note: mB here means **millibucket**, i.e. **one thousandth of a bucket**, **not** the **MB** used as a **computer data unit**) make 4 polymer bars.

To find an oil field, I crafted an oil detector. After a great deal of effort, hard work finally paid off and I found one.

For convenience, I built a refinery right next to the oil field. Once a boiler heats up the crude oil, refining can begin.

![The first oil field. Nothing has changed apart from replacing the oil derrick with a more efficient pumpjack](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil1.png)

Refining regular crude oil yields heavy oil, naphtha, light oil and petroleum gas, plus sulfur as a solid byproduct.

At first, since I was making polymer bars, only petroleum gas was really useful. As for the leftover heavy oil, naphtha and light oil, I fractioned them into heating oil, diesel and kerosene, and then just burned them.

These things would become useful later, but back then distant water couldn't put out a nearby fire.

The solar tower boiler doesn't generate power at night, and fixing the nighttime power shortage was urgent, so I had no choice but to build an industrial combustion engine to burn all of it.

Of course, not every fractioning product got burned, since some of them can't be burned at all. I stored those in fluid barrels.

The bitumen was originally meant for paving roads, but I never got around to it before the world was finished.

The lubricant couldn't be burned and I had no use for it at the time, so I stored it too.

## A Durable Rare Earth Alloy
In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, Desh is an alloy made of various rare earth metals. It's very useful in the game, but it's fictional.

The early shredder, and the centrifuge I built after the oil industry got going, already improved ore processing. But as later industries needed more and more ore, improving ore processing further became a top priority.

So building an ore acidizer became my next task. The ore acidizer treats ores with acid to crystallize them, and putting the crystals into a centrifuge increases the yield.

But the ore acidizer needs Desh ingots. To make Desh ingots in the chemical plant, I needed Desh blend, light oil (yes, the same stuff I'd been burning) and mercury.

To get Desh blend, you shred rare earth ore chunks (which drop when you mine rare earth ore) in the shredder, and rare earth ore isn't easy to find. It took me quite a while to collect enough rare earth ore chunks.

Getting mercury wasn't simple either. I had to go deep underground (close to bedrock) to find deep cinnabar ore, and then put the cinnabar into the coke oven.

Deep ores are very hard. Ordinary pickaxes can't mine them (you can only blow them up), so I also made mining charges for this.

After a thoroughly satisfying round of blasting, I'd collected enough cinnabar.

Desh isn't only for the ore acidizer. It's also used for the hydraulic fracking tower (for tapping bedrock oil deposits for endless oil), the catalytic cracking tower (more on that later), the gas centrifuge (for enriching uranium-235) and other machines.

What's more, Desh tools have infinite durability, which mattered a lot to me (especially the pickaxe). Even though the Desh pickaxe only mines at the same level as an iron pickaxe, its strengths far outweigh that flaw, and I could finally dig mineshafts to get more ore.

![The mineshafts I later dug with the Desh pickaxe](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/mineshafts.png)

![Inside the mineshafts](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/mineshafts_inner.png)

## The Oil Industry Goes Further
Later, I built a catalytic cracking tower and pumpjacks, found another oil field, and started exploring the other route of the oil industry.

![The second oil field. It has run dry, so the pumpjack has been removed](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil2.png)

Cracked crude oil gives different products when refined: cracked naphtha, cracked light oil, aromatic hydrocarbons and unsaturated hydrocarbons.

At this point, cracked naphtha and cracked light oil got almost the same treatment that heavy oil and naphtha got early on: I fractioned them into heating oil, cracked diesel and kerosene, and burned them all.

One difference, though: fractioning cracked light oil also gives petroleum gas, which regular light oil doesn't.

Aromatic and unsaturated hydrocarbons are both crucial.

The former can be combined with petroleum gas to make bakelite bars, a cheaper replacement for polymer bars.

The latter can be combined with sulfur to make rubber bars.

Both the ZIRNOX and the PWR nuclear reactors need rubber bars, and so do machines like the electric press, the industrial steam turbine and the cyclotron.

## The Start of the Nuclear Industry
As my base became more and more industrialized, the solar tower boiler and the coal-fired thermal power station alone were no longer enough to keep the machines running.

So a "more advanced" way of "boiling water" became especially important, which opened the chapter on nuclear power plants.

At first, my base ran on the ZIRNOX, a nuclear reactor in HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod modeled on the Magnox reactor (a first-generation reactor design).

This reactor is fairly cheap to build and generates a decent amount of power.

It uses carbon dioxide as coolant to carry heat away from the core and pass it on to water. In other words, it's just another way of boiling water.

In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, steam comes in four grades: steam, dense steam, super dense steam and ultra dense steam. Steam turbines turn higher-grade steam into lower-grade steam, until ordinary steam becomes "low-pressure steam", at which point it needs a cooling tower to turn back into water and complete the water cycle.

Since the ZIRNOX outputs super dense steam, I hooked up three steam turbines.

Once the ZIRNOX was up and running, I finally didn't have to worry about power at night.

![The ZIRNOX](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/zirnox.png)

## Extracting the Essence
For a nuclear reactor, using natural uranium directly as fuel isn't always a good idea. To enrich uranium-235, I built four gas centrifuges (four of them in series are needed to separate out pure uranium-235).

However, gas centrifuges can't separate uranium-235 straight from uranium ingots. The uranium first has to be made into yellowcake, and then into gaseous uranium hexafluoride.

Once the gas centrifuges separate the uranium hexafluoride, you get a lot of uranium-238 and a little uranium-235.

Uranium-238 isn't as suitable as uranium-235 for direct use as conventional fission fuel, but it still has plenty of uses, such as crafting hazardous material tanks (which hold small amounts of dangerous fluids), containment boxes (put radioactive items inside to block their radiation) and ducrete (a high-strength building material).

Still, uranium-235 makes up only a small fraction of natural uranium, and I didn't have bedrock ore yet, so I didn't use uranium-235 directly to make highly enriched uranium-235 fuel rods.

Mixing uranium-235 and uranium-238 in a certain ratio gives medium-enriched uranium fuel.

Uranium fuel can be used directly as nuclear fuel, or mixed with plutonium-239 to make MOX fuel.

Of course, uranium-238 can also be mixed with reactor-grade plutonium (a mix of plutonium-239 and plutonium-240) to make lower-concentration plutonium fuel.

The only problem was that I didn't have any plutonium yet, so that was out of the question.

## The Transformation of Nuclear Fuel
What can you do with spent nuclear fuel? Once processed, quite a lot!

I'd been worrying about where to get plutonium, so I built a PUREX to process spent nuclear fuel.

Of course, you can't just throw spent fuel into the PUREX. It also needs kerosene and nitric acid. Nitric acid can be made from niter + sulfuric acid, and kerosene comes from fractioning light oil or cracked light oil, but I'd burned all of that.

Fortunately, after some fiddling around, I finally extracted a little reactor-grade plutonium, as well as technetium-99, from spent uranium fuel.

I've already mentioned what plutonium is for. Technetium-99 can be combined with steel to make technetium steel. This alloy is needed for everything from technetium steel barrels (which hold highly corrosive fluids) to the vacuum refinery, the hydrotreater, the Leviathan steam turbine and huge storage tanks.

But technetium-99 was still scarce at the time, and processing spent uranium fuel alone clearly couldn't meet later demand.

Luckily, with plutonium in hand, I had another way to get technetium-99: the ZIRNOX's ZFB (*Zirconium Fast Breeding*, a series of fast breeding fuel rods) MOX fuel rods.

These fuel rods produce relatively little heat, so naturally they don't generate much power, but the upside is that you can fill the whole reactor with them without worrying much about a meltdown.

More importantly, ZFB MOX fuel rods deplete quickly, and processing those depleted rods for technetium-99 was exactly what I needed.

Regular MOX fuel rods aren't bad either, but I didn't have enough plutonium at the time. In comparison, I preferred using ZFB MOX fuel rods to produce technetium-99.

## Element 126
Schrabidium-326 (Sa326) is another fictional resource in HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod. It's supposedly a superheavy element sitting on the island of stability, but in the game its radioactivity is anything but weak. It can even blind you: without protective gear, it's easy to lose your sight.

Getting Sa326 is no easy feat either. It takes a lot of material (mainly uranium ore), and a lot of luck.

Processing spent nuclear fuel produces some nuclear waste, which can be packed into nuclear waste barrels.

Detonating a nuclear waste barrel with explosives creates a radiation zone, and uranium ore caught in it has a tiny chance of turning into Sa326 ore.

When I tried it, only 5 out of several stacks of uranium ore were converted. Looks like I really am not that lucky.

Sa326 is mainly used to make nuclear fuel, but it has other uses too, such as making more advanced gear.

Still, since this Sa326 had been so hard to come by, I didn't rush to use it. Instead, I acidized and centrifuged it (to increase the yield) and stored it.

Before building the fusion reactor, I had no way to get ionized particles, so I had no chance to turn this Sa326 into schrabidic acid.

Schrabidic acid is needed to extract Sa326 from spent plutonium fuel. Since it turns a little into a lot, I also call this "duplicating".

Of course, this process also needs organic solvent, which needs naphtha and aromatic hydrocarbons. Sadly, I'd fractioned all my naphtha into diesel and heating oil and burned it.

So this Sa326 couldn't be put to use for the time being. After all, I didn't want to kill the goose that lays the golden eggs; that wouldn't be worth it.

## A Little Chernobyl
The RBMK is one of the most important nuclear reactors in HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod. The Soviet Union's Chernobyl nuclear power plant also used the RBMK.

An RBMK meltdown is extremely violent, but the RBMK not only generates a lot of power, it can also produce important resources through its irradiation channels: for example, irradiating thorium-232 to make thorium fuel (which can be used directly in nuclear reactors), irradiating chlorinated paraffin to make high-performance solvent (needed for processing bedrock ore), and irradiating lithium to make tritium (one of the main fusion fuels).

To push bedrock ore processing forward as soon as possible, and to take the oil industry further, there was no getting around high-performance solvent, so I built an RBMK.

Of course, the RBMK produces huge amounts of ultra dense steam, which industrial steam turbines can't handle. So at the same time, I also built four Leviathan steam turbines.

![The first RBMK reactor, later only fitted with fuel autoloaders, right next to the vertical farm](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/rbmk1.png)

Conditions were fairly tough at first, so I could only use uranium fuel billets to make medium-enriched uranium-235 fuel rods.

Later, once things got better, to balance irradiation and power generation, I first mixed medium-enriched uranium-235 fuel rods with MOX fuel rods, and finally switched entirely to MOX fuel rods.

Although the RBMK's power decays over time, a starved camel is still bigger than a horse: the RBMK can feed the grid for hours on end, which is better than the ZIRNOX.

The one drawback was that lithium-ion batteries don't store much energy, so a lot of power went to waste. Only after the cyclotron came online did all that surplus power finally find a use.

To get high-performance solvent through irradiation, I first needed to make chlorinated paraffin from chlorine and paraffin wax.

Paraffin wax isn't hard to get. Fractioning heavy oil gives bitumen (yes, the same bitumen that never got used for roads) and industrial oil. Fractioning the industrial oil again gives heating oil and lubricant, and solidifying the lubricant gives paraffin wax.

But where does chlorine come from? Sodalite! I was going to make sodium-iron batteries later (one tier above lithium-ion batteries) anyway, so I acidized the lapis lazuli ore in my storage first.

Acidizing gives lapis crystals, and putting them into a centrifuge gives some sodalite. Putting sodalite into the coke oven then gives sodium and chlorine, both of which I needed.

With high-performance solvent sorted, I started on mining and processing bedrock ore.

## Inexhaustible
In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, bedrock ore is tedious to process, but it provides an inexhaustible supply of ore.

Bedrock ores spawned in different chunks have different parameters, and these parameters determine how abundant each component is in the raw ore mined there.

The first bedrock ore I found was right near my home. Sorted by amount, its components were rare earth (2.71), actinides (2.68), light metals (1.91), heavy metals (1.16), crystals (0.79) and non-metals (0.16). These numbers show how much of each component you get, on average, per raw ore processed.

![The first bedrock ore drill](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/drill1.png)

To take the oil industry further, the first thing I processed was the heavy metal component. It's not a large share, but it's not ridiculously small either.

After treating the heavy metal component with high-performance solvent, I went through a series of tedious processing steps and finally got bismuth, tantalum and gold. Bismuth was the mineral I needed to push the oil industry forward.

Of course, I processed the other bedrock ore components later on too. To hold all those batches of final products, I built a mass-production warehouse.

The mass-production warehouse is divided into several zones, each with several mass storage units.

In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, a mass storage unit stores large quantities of a single kind of item. Each zone of the mass-production warehouse is laid out 6 × 5, which means each zone can store up to 30 kinds of items.

The final products from bedrock ore all went into zone 102-1 (refined minerals of all kinds).

From then on, whenever some resource reached mass production and no longer fit in the old storage, the mass-production warehouse came in handy.

![The mass-production warehouse as it looked back then](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/mass_storage.png)

## Freeing My Hands
Sometimes you need to feed a big batch of items into a machine, but each recipe takes time to process, and feeding items in while you wait is very inefficient.

This is where pneumatic tubes really shine: they can move items from a container into a machine, and the other way round, from a machine into a container.

But pneumatic tubes need compressed air to shoot items through.

So I set up an air intake and a compressor in my base's air distribution room, dedicated to pressurizing these pneumatic tubes.

![The air distribution room, with a boiler that feeds a hot air blast into the blast furnace](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/air_dist_room.png)

Take the ore acidizer: I wanted to process several stacks of ore, but it only holds one stack at a time. Every time it finished one stack, I had to put in another myself, and take the products out first.

With pneumatic tubes, though, I can put two chests next to the machine: one holds the ore, which the tubes shoot into the machine, and the other receives the products the machine shoots out.

![An ore acidizer with pneumatic tubes](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/acidizer_with_tube.png)

## The Power of Lasers
The RBMK differs from the ZIRNOX in one way: its spent fuel has to be processed in the laser isotope separation chamber (SILEX) instead of the PUREX.

The SILEX itself doesn't need power, but it needs a laser aimed at it, and the laser does need power.

The laser can hold different kinds of laser crystals. At first, only the infrared crystal is available.

Besides processing spent RBMK fuel, the SILEX has another advantage: it can enrich uranium-235 in bulk much more efficiently.

In the SILEX, uranium ingots don't have to be made into yellowcake, then uranium hexafluoride, before enriching uranium-235.

Of course, separating isotopes in the SILEX requires hydrogen peroxide.

The bedrock ore near my home happened to be rich in actinides, so I used liquid hydrogen to raise the share of its heavy fraction. The heavy fraction here is mainly uranium, while the light fraction is thorium.

Combined with pneumatic tubes, I could put batches of uranium ingots into a chest. That way I only had to leave the game running while I slept, and in the morning I'd find the chests full of uranium-235 and uranium-238.


## Under Vacuum
Now that I had bismuth, what next? I used bismuth nuggets to make versatile integrated circuits, then versatile circuit boards, and finally a vacuum refinery.

Since the hydraulic fracking tower had yet to make an appearance, I gave it its chance here.

![The hydraulic fracking tower on the first bedrock oil deposit](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil3-1.png)

After all, I'd need plenty of oil products later on, and at the time I also planned to relocate my other production lines and set them up with hydraulic fracking towers.

Of course, its debut didn't come free.

First I used the oil detector to find a bedrock oil deposit at least 300 m away from home, because the hydraulic fracking tower pollutes the environment and makes the land ugly.

The fracking fluid it needs isn't hard to make either: just sulfuric acid and petroleum gas.

Unlike the regular refinery, in HBM the vacuum refinery doesn't need the oil to be heated. Instead, the oil has to be pressurized to 2 PU.

![The refining setup for the first bedrock oil deposit, including the compressor, the vacuum refinery and the later-added hydrotreater](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil3-2.png)

Processing regular crude oil in the vacuum refinery gives vacuum heavy oil, reformate, vacuum light oil and sour gas.

What I valued most was reformate, because fractioning it gives aromatic hydrocarbons and BTX.

I've already mentioned what aromatic hydrocarbons are for. BTX can be combined with phosgene (made from chlorine and unsaturated hydrocarbons) to make hard plastic bars.

This material is very useful in the late game: sodium-iron batteries need it, and so do the pyrolysis oven, the fusion reactor and the particle accelerator.

![The huge storage tanks for the first bedrock oil deposit, added later](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil3-3.png)

However, hard plastic bars aren't the only option. There's a substitute: PVC bars.

PVC bars don't need BTX, but they do need cadmium powder, which can be obtained from mustard willow leaves.

Interestingly, mustard willow has to absorb environmental pollution to mature.

Since the hydraulic fracking tower pollutes anyway, I built a mustard willow farm near the oil field, trying to put that pollution to use.

![The mustard willow farm](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/mustard_willow_farm.png)

Unfortunately, the mustard willow farm wasn't very efficient, and in the end I only made a handful of PVC bars.

## Cohesion
Lots of things need ionized particles, such as schrabidic acid and quantum batteries.

But where do ionized particles come from? You need a fusion reactor.

Using tritium and helium-4 as fusion fuel gives ionized particles.

Tritium isn't too hard to get; it mainly comes from irradiating lithium in the RBMK.

But what about helium-4? Helium-4 also needs a fusion reactor, plus deuterium.

To get deuterium, I built a deuterium extraction tower, which extracts heavy water from water.

Electrolyzing heavy water gives a little deuterium.

Since all of this depended on a fusion reactor, I decided to go all in and threw myself into building one.

![The fusion reactor when it was nearly finished](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/fusion1.png)

A fusion reactor can't be used the moment it's built; it needs a large amount of power to start up.

It also needs cold perfluoromethane for cooling, which is perfluoromethane pressurized to 2 PU.

Perfluoromethane needs petroleum gas, unsaturated hydrocarbons and fluorite, and the first two happen to be products of catalytic cracking.

As for power, I worried the old RBMK might not be able to handle it, so I later built a PWR.

![The PWR](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/pwr.png)

The best coolant for the PWR is liquid sodium, so I processed the light metal component of the bedrock ore and put the sodium I got from it into the liquefactor.

Liquid sodium sorted, but what about fuel rods?

In the PWR, medium-enriched uranium-235 fuel rods don't work well, but I still needed to keep my uranium-235.

Since I also had quite a lot of thorium-232, I put it into the RBMK's irradiation channels to turn it into thorium fuel.

I later used this thorium fuel in the ZIRNOX, and once it was spent, the PUREX could recover uranium-233 from it.

Then I made that uranium-233 into PWR fuel rods.

With the power and raw material problems solved, I started on helium-4.

To get helium-4, you can use deuterium alone as fusion fuel, or deuterium and tritium, which halves the deuterium consumption.

Once you have helium-4, using tritium and helium-4 as fusion fuel gives ionized particles.

With ionized particles, I could finally start duplicating Sa326.

A steady stream of Sa326 let me craft a set of schrabidium armor.

Schrabidium armor has great radiation resistance, and it gives buffs too.

I made part of the remaining Sa326 into low-enriched schrabidium fuel and stored the rest.

Later, I connected two fusion reactor vessels in series and added an MHD generator, and it could finally generate power.

![The fusion reactor after being connected in series](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/fusion2.png)

## The Strongest Fuel on Earth
In HBM's Nuclear Tech Mod, balefire rocket fuel is the strongest of all fluid fuels.

But making balefire rocket fuel needs kerosene and balefire egg shards, and balefire egg shards need thermonuclear ashes.

How do you get thermonuclear ashes? The fusion reactor can make them from balefire rocket fuel and antimatter, but that turns into a chicken-and-egg problem.

Later I looked it up: you deplete an RBMK flashgold fuel rod, then break it down in the SILEX to get thermonuclear ashes.

Making a flashgold billet takes a gold-198 billet and antimatter.

Gold-198 ingots aren't hard to get; you just irradiate gold ingots in the RBMK.

Antimatter is a bit harder.

Antimatter usually comes as a byproduct of the cyclotron, or from the particle accelerator, but I hadn't built a particle accelerator yet.

On top of that, storing antimatter needs a magnetic antimatter containment cell, or it explodes.

Once I'd sorted out the containment cells, I didn't know what to use the cyclotron for.

But since schrabidium fuel needs neptunium, I just kept making neptunium powder from uranium powder and lithium powder cubes.

After collecting some antimatter, I started making flashgold billets.

Next, I made them into RBMK fuel rods.

But at the time I must have had a brain fart: I put them right next to highly enriched uranium-235 fuel rods, and the reactor blew up.

I had no choice but to load a backup, losing some progress.

This time I was smarter and put them next to low-enriched australium fuel rods (the number of neutrons these fuel rods release doesn't grow without bound), and it finally ran.

I left the game running for a whole day and night, and finally used them up.

After that, I used the thermonuclear ashes from breaking them down to make balefire egg shards, then mixed them with kerosene to get balefire rocket fuel.

Since I also had a jetpack, I used up the remaining fuel in it and refilled it with balefire rocket fuel.

I have to say, it flies a lot faster. No wonder it's the strongest fuel.

I planned to put the remaining balefire rocket fuel into the fusion reactor, but then I found I didn't have enough antimatter.

## Sweetness in the Sour
Reformate gas is needed for the high-performance solvent used in processing bedrock ore and extracting fullerenes, the welded CMB steel plates the ICF needs, the welded osmiridium plates needed in the late game, and making saturnite ingots.

I used to get reformate gas mainly by fractioning vacuum light oil, but that clearly wasn't enough for my needs.

Later I brought in the hydrotreater. Hydrodesulfurization produces desulfurized crude oil as its main product, plus sour gas as a byproduct.

Processing desulfurized crude oil in the vacuum refinery also yields reformate gas, which boosted my reformate gas output further.

By the way, the high-sulfur natural gas is called *sour gas* because it contains a lot of hydrogen sulfide.

Later, I found another bedrock oil deposit, set it up with a hydrotreater and a catalytic cracking tower, and started processing desulfurized crude oil.

![The desulfurized oil field](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/desulfurized_oil_refining.png)

This route yields desulfurized naphtha, desulfurized light oil, aromatic hydrocarbons and unsaturated hydrocarbons.

Desulfurized naphtha can be fractioned into BTX and high-cetane diesel, while desulfurized light oil can be fractioned into high-cetane diesel and jet fuel.

For me, BTX was the more useful one. As for those fuels, they don't perform as well as balefire rocket fuel, but at least they can still be burned, so they're not wasted.

But just expanding the oil fields wasn't enough. To make better use of all the oil products, I also built an oil processing base, with a radiolysis chamber, a catalytic reformer, a coker unit, and multiple fractioning towers for different recipes.

![Facilities at the oil processing base](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil_processing_base1.png)

![The fractioning towers at the oil processing base](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/oil_processing_base2.png)

The radiolysis chamber is a special one.

It uses RTG pellets to crack oil products. It's only half as fast as the catalytic cracking tower, but it generates power at the same time.

As it happened, a lot of polonium had piled up in the mass-production warehouse from processing actinide bedrock ore earlier. So I made that polonium into RTG pellets for the radiolysis chamber.

It's not the most efficient option, but at least it put that stockpile to use.

The coker unit is valuable too. It turns oil products into petroleum coke and, depending on the recipe, also produces coker oil, coker naphtha or coker gas.

Coker gas is one of the ingredients for weapon steel, which is exactly what I'd need later to make weapons and take on bosses.

Besides that, the coker unit can also get reformate by processing vacuum heavy oil, which was good news for me too.

As for the catalytic reformer, it can turn one oil product into a more useful one.

For example, it can turn naphtha into reformate, or turn sour gas into sulfuric acid.

What's more, whichever recipe it runs, it also produces a little liquid hydrogen. That liquid hydrogen can go into the hydrotreater or be saved for other uses.

And so, I gradually built up multiple oil processing routes.

In particular, by cracking reformate and running desulfurized crude oil through the vacuum refinery, I finally broke free of relying mainly on fractioning vacuum light oil for reformate gas.

## Approaching the Speed of Light
Making more balefire rocket fuel needs a lot of thermonuclear ashes, and getting them in quantity means relying on the fusion reactor.

But where would the antimatter come from? The old cyclotron did give antimatter, but far too little.

That's when I thought of the particle accelerator.

Building a particle accelerator is no easy task; it takes a huge amount of material.

![The particle accelerator as it first looked](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/accelerator1.png)

The initial single-stage particle accelerator was enough to produce antimatter.

Producing antimatter in the particle accelerator only takes copper powder, liquid hydrogen and ionized particles, and it uses noticeably less power than the cyclotron.

Not only that, the cyclotron gives at most 150 mB of antimatter per recipe, whereas the particle accelerator produces 1000 mB each time.

But the particles needed to irradiate uranium-238 ingots into Sa326, as well as the DNT the ICF needs, both required expanding the particle accelerator further.

So I kept expanding it, and built a simple redstone selector that uses two levers to represent four states: 00, 01, 10 and 11.

![The particle accelerator's redstone selector](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/accelerator_selector.png)

That way, I could decide which stage the particles should exit from.

But then I found a problem: the old redstone selector could only decide which stage the particles exit from; it couldn't adjust the particles' target momentum automatically.

This gave me a bit of a headache, because every time I ran a different recipe, I had to adjust the dipole magnets' target momentum myself, which was a real hassle.

Later I found out that dipole magnets can be controlled by OpenComputers, so I took the chance to explore OpenComputers.

After crafting the parts I needed, I modified the old redstone selector so that a control system decides which line to activate.

![The particle accelerator's control system](@/assets/images/hbm-ntm/accelerator_controller.png)

But I'm not familiar with Lua, so I explained what I needed to ChatGPT and had it write the control script for me.

As the actual situation kept changing, I also had ChatGPT revise the control script several times to fit my requirements.

With the control system in place, I only have to enter the target momentum, and it automatically adjusts the dipole magnets' parameters and operates the redstone selector to decide which stage the particles exit from.

## Endless Energy

The DFC can use lots of fuel combinations (it can even run on a single fuel). I chose antischrabidium and balefire rocket fuel. The former is the strongest fuel for the DFC (2.7× reaction multiplier), and the latter comes right after it (2.5× reaction multiplier), and I happened to have a lot of it at home.

Compared with pure antischrabidium, this combination generates a bit less power, but the DFC's output is already ridiculous, and this way it uses less antischrabidium.

## Extra 5: Making the Most of Everything
Looking at the radioactive hazards piled up at home, I wondered: could they be made more useful?
